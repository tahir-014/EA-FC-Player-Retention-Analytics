# EA FC Player Retention & Onboarding Analytics

## 1. Product Context

EA FC is a live-service football gaming experience where player engagement depends on the quality of the onboarding experience, gameplay, progression, events, social features, and ongoing content.

For this project, we will analyze a synthetic dataset representing new-player behavior during the early stages of their journey.

The goal is to understand how players move through the onboarding and early engagement funnel and identify behavioral patterns associated with retention and churn.


## 2. Problem Statement

New players may drop out during the early stages of their EA FC journey before developing a habit of returning to the game.

 As a product team needs to understand:

- Where players drop off during onboarding and early engagement
- Which player behaviors are associated with retention
- Which player segments have higher churn
- What potential friction points may be contributing to player drop-off
- Which product opportunities could improve early player retention


## 3. Target Users

### Primary User

New EA FC players who have recently started playing the game.

### Focus Segment

Players during their early lifecycle, particularly the period from acquisition through the first 30 days.

### Product Team Stakeholders

The analysis is intended to support decision-making by:

- Product Managers
- Game Designers
- Data Analysts
- User Researchers
- Marketing/Growth Teams
- Engineering Teams


## 4. Potential Business Impact

Early player retention is important for a live-service game because players who continue returning have more opportunities to engage with gameplay, progression systems, events, social features, and other game experiences.

Improving the early player journey could potentially:

- Increase Day-1 and Day-7 retention
- Increase player engagement
- Reduce early churn
- Improve progression through the game
- Increase participation in live events and social features
- Create stronger long-term player relationships


## 5. Project Objective

The objective of this project is to analyze new-player onboarding and early-game behavior to identify major player drop-off points, understand factors associated with retention and churn, and recommend data-driven product improvements.

The analysis will focus primarily on Day-7 retention as the key early lifecycle success metric.


## 6. Key Product Questions

### Player Journey

1. How do new players progress through the onboarding funnel?
2. At which stage do we observe the largest player drop-off?
3. What percentage of players complete the core early-game journey?

### Retention

4. What are the overall Day-1, Day-7, and Day-30 retention rates?
5. How does retention differ across player segments?
6. Does onboarding completion correlate with higher retention?

### Engagement

7. How do sessions, matches, session duration, and progression relate to retention?
8. Which behaviors are associated with highly engaged players?

### Churn

9. Which player segments have the highest churn?
10. What behaviors are associated with players who churn?

### Product Opportunities

11. What potential friction points can be identified from the data?
12. What product improvements could address these friction points?
13. How could these improvements be tested through an A/B experiment?


## 7. Primary Success Metric

### Day-7 Retention (D7)

Day-7 retention measures the percentage of newly acquired players who return to the game seven days after their initial acquisition.

Formula:

D7 Retention = Players who return on Day 7 / Total eligible new players × 100


## 8. Secondary Metrics

The analysis will also track:

### Retention

- Day-30 Retention

### Engagement
- Sessions per player
- Matches per player

### Onboarding

- First-match completion rate

### Progression

- Rewards claimed

### Churn
- Overall churn rate
- Churn by player segment

### Social Engagement
- Social feature adoption

⚠️ Limitations
This is a synthetic portfolio project.
The analysis does not represent:
- EA internal telemetry
- EA player behavior
- EA experiments
- EA product strategy
- EA proprietary data
Observational relationships should not be interpreted as causal.
The A/B experiment demonstrates experimentation methodology using synthetic data and would require validation with real production telemetry.



## 10. Data Disclaimer

This is a portfolio project created to demonstrate Product Management and Product Analytics skills.

The dataset used in this project is synthetic and is not official Electronic Arts or EA SPORTS FC player telemetry.

No proprietary EA data is used.

The analysis, findings, hypotheses, and recommendations are intended for educational and portfolio purposes.
