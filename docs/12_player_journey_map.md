# Player Journey Map

## EA FC New Player — First 7 Days

> Portfolio concept based on synthetic player analytics. No proprietary EA or EA SPORTS FC player data was used.

---

# 1. Journey Objective

The purpose of the journey map is to understand how a new player moves from initial entry into the game toward repeat engagement and Day-7 retention.

The journey focuses on the first week because early behavior is strongly associated with D7 retention in the synthetic dataset.

The journey is structured around:

**Discovery → Onboarding → First Match → Early Engagement → Progression → Reward → Return → D7**

---

# 2. Player Persona

## Target Player

**Moderately Engaged New Player**

### Profile

- New to the game
- 4–10 sessions during Week 1
- 45.02% of the player population
- 22,511 players
- 27.16% D7 retention

### Core Need

The player needs clear reasons to understand what to do next, make meaningful progress, and return to the game.

# 4. Stage 1 — Discovery

## Player Goal

Understand what the game offers and decide whether to continue.

## Player Action

The player enters the game after acquisition.

## Product Experience

The player encounters the initial game experience and begins the new-player journey.

## Potential Friction

The player may not immediately understand:

- What they should do first
- What the game expects them to learn
- What they can achieve during the first session

## Product Opportunity

Set clear expectations and establish a simple initial path.

## Metric

**New Players**

---

# 5. Stage 2 — Onboarding

## Player Goal

Understand the game and basic mechanics.

## Player Action

The player starts or completes the tutorial.

## Evidence

Tutorial completion:

**75.54%**

D7 retention among tutorial completers:

**39.03%**

D7 retention among non-completers:

**13.09%**

Difference:

**25.94 pp**

## Potential Friction

Players who do not complete onboarding may fail to develop enough understanding or motivation to continue.

## Product Opportunity

Make onboarding clear, useful, and connected to the player's next gameplay action.

## Metric

**Tutorial Completion Rate**

---

# 6. Stage 3 — First Match

## Player Goal

Experience meaningful gameplay.

## Player Action

The player enters and completes their first match.

## Evidence

Players who played their first match:

**40.24% D7 retention**

Players who did not:

**9.91% D7 retention**

Difference:

**30.33 pp**

## Potential Friction

Players may fail to reach meaningful gameplay after entering the game.

## Product Opportunity

Reduce friction between onboarding and first meaningful match participation.

## Metric

**First-Match Conversion Rate**

---

# 7. Stage 4 — Early Engagement

## Player Goal

Decide whether the game is worth returning to.

## Player Action

The player participates in additional sessions and matches.

## Evidence

D7 retention by engagement:

| Segment | D7 Retention |
|---|---:|
| Low | 15.75% |
| Moderate | 27.16% |
| High | 44.27% |
| Very High | 67.34% |

## Key Insight

Engagement is strongly associated with D7 retention.

The Moderate segment is especially important because it represents:

**45.02% of all players**

## Potential Friction

Players may participate occasionally without developing a strong reason to return.

## Product Opportunity

Provide clear short-term goals and meaningful reasons to continue playing.

## Metrics

- Sessions per player
- Matches per player
- Second-session conversion
- D7 retention

---

# 8. Stage 5 — Progression

## Player Goal

Feel that their time in the game is producing meaningful progress.

## Player Action

The player completes matches and activities that contribute to progression.

## Evidence

Moderate Engagement:

**8.37 average progression**

High Engagement:

**18.12 average progression**

Difference:

**9.75 progression points**

## Potential Friction

Players may not clearly understand:

- What they are progressing toward
- How close they are to the next milestone
- Why completing another activity matters

## Product Opportunity

Make early progression visible and connected to achievable goals.

## Metric

**Average Progression Level**

---

# 9. Stage 6 — Reward

## Player Goal

Receive meaningful value from continued participation.

## Player Action

The player completes activities and claims rewards.

## Evidence

Moderate Engagement:

**3.04 average rewards**

High Engagement:

**6.33 average rewards**

Difference:

**3.29 rewards**

## Potential Friction

Rewards may not provide enough motivation to continue the journey.

## Product Opportunity

Connect rewards directly to short-term objectives and visible milestones.

## Metric

**Rewards Claimed per Player**

---

# 10. Stage 7 — Return

## Player Goal

Know why they should come back.

## Player Action

The player starts another session.

## Product Experience

The player sees available activities, objectives, progression, and rewards.

## Potential Friction

The player may have completed their previous activity without having a compelling next step.

## Product Opportunity

Always provide a clear next action.

### Example

**Objective Completed**

↓

**Reward Earned**

↓

**Progress Updated**

↓

**Next Objective Recommended**

↓

**Reason to Return**

## Metrics

- Second-session conversion
- Sessions per player
- Matches per player

---

# 11. Stage 8 — Day-7 Retention

## Player Goal

Continue engaging with the game.

## Player Action

The player returns on Day 7.

## Primary KPI

**D7 Retention**

Overall:

**32.68%**

## Product Goal

Increase the percentage of new players who develop a repeat-play habit.

---

# 12. Journey Pain Point

The strongest strategic pain point identified in the analysis is the transition from **initial engagement to stronger repeat engagement**.

The Moderate Engagement segment represents a large player population but has substantially lower D7 retention than High Engagement players.

### Moderate

- 22,511 players
- 45.02% player share
- 27.16% D7
- 7.00 sessions
- 7.88 matches
- 8.37 progression
- 3.04 rewards

### High

- 12,885 players
- 25.77% player share
- 44.27% D7
- 15.04 sessions
- 17.27 matches
- 18.12 progression
- 6.33 rewards

---

# 13. Key Journey Insight

The largest differences between Moderate and High Engagement players are:

1. Repeat sessions
2. Match participation
3. Progression
4. Rewards

Average session duration is almost identical:

- Moderate: 39.88 minutes
- High: 39.92 minutes

This suggests the product opportunity may be less about making sessions longer and more about creating stronger reasons to **return and participate in meaningful gameplay**.

# 17. Journey Experiment

The improved journey should be tested through a randomized A/B experiment.

## Control

Existing new-player experience.

## Treatment

Week 1 Player Journey.

## Primary Measurement

D7 retention.

## Secondary Measurement

- Sessions
- Matches
- Progression
- Rewards
- Objective completion
- Journey completion
- Second-session conversion
- Social adoption

## Guardrails

Monitor technical and player-experience metrics to ensure that increased engagement does not create negative side effects.

---

# 18. Final Journey Principle

> **The goal is not simply to make players play more. The goal is to help players discover meaningful reasons to play, progress, earn value, and return.**

This distinction is important because the product should optimize for sustainable player value rather than simply increasing session length.
