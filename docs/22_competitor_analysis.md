# Competitor & Market Analysis

## EA FC Player Retention & Onboarding Analytics

> Portfolio analysis based on publicly observable product patterns and PM hypotheses. This analysis does not claim access to proprietary EA or competitor data.

---

# 1. Objective

The purpose of this analysis is to understand how competing and adjacent sports/gaming experiences create:

- Early engagement
- Progression
- Player goals
- Rewards
- Social interaction
- Repeat-play motivation
- Long-term retention

The goal is not to copy competitors.

The goal is to identify product patterns that could inform hypotheses for improving the new-player experience.

# 5. Competitive Pattern: Clear Goals

A common pattern across successful live-service games is the use of clear short-term goals.

Examples include:

- Daily objectives
- Weekly objectives
- Seasonal goals
- Challenges
- Milestones
- Progress trackers

### Product Insight

Players should understand:

> "What can I accomplish next?"

This directly connects with the Moderate Engagement problem identified in this project.

---

# 6. Competitive Pattern: Visible Progression

Progression creates a visible connection between:

```text
PLAYER ACTION
      ↓
PROGRESS
      ↓
REWARD
      ↓
NEW GOAL
```

For new players, progression should be:

- Easy to understand
- Visible
- Meaningful
- Connected to gameplay

---

# 7. Competitive Pattern: Multiple Reasons to Return

Strong live-service experiences can provide multiple return motivations:

### Gameplay

"I want to play another match."

### Progression

"I want to level up."

### Rewards

"I want to unlock the reward."

### Competition

"I want to improve my performance."

### Social

"I want to play with friends."

### Content

"I want to experience the new event."

A strong player journey does not depend on only one motivation.

---

# 8. Competitive Pattern: Live Events

Live events can create temporary urgency and freshness.

Examples of event mechanics include:

- Limited-time objectives
- Seasonal progression
- Special challenges
- New content
- Competitive events
- Community events

### PM Consideration

Live events should provide meaningful player value rather than simply increasing activity metrics.

---

# 9. Competitive Pattern: Social Motivation

Social features can create additional reasons to return.

Potential social motivations:

- Playing with friends
- Clubs/teams
- Competitive matchmaking
- Leaderboards
- Shared goals
- Community events

However:

> Social features should be relevant to the player's motivation and should not be forced on every player.

# 11. Competitive Gap Hypothesis

Based on this project's synthetic analytics, a potential opportunity is:

> New players may benefit from a more structured early journey that connects gameplay, progression, rewards, and clear next steps.

This is a **product hypothesis**, not a claim that EA FC currently lacks these systems.

---

# 13. Differentiation Principle

The goal should not be:

> "Copy what competitors do."

Instead:

> "Understand why a product pattern works and determine whether the same player need exists in our product."

For example:

A competitor may use daily objectives.

The PM question is not:

> "Should we copy daily objectives?"

The better question is:

> "Do our players need clearer short-term goals, and would solving that problem improve the player experience?"

---

# 14. Competitive Research Questions

A PM could investigate:

### Onboarding

- How quickly can a new player reach meaningful gameplay?
- What information is introduced first?
- How much choice is given to the player?

### Progression

- How clearly is progress communicated?
- What happens after a milestone?
- How quickly does the player see meaningful advancement?

### Rewards

- What actions generate rewards?
- Are rewards predictable?
- Are rewards connected to player goals?

### Social

- When are social features introduced?
- Are they optional?
- What player problem do they solve?

### Live Events

- How frequently does new content appear?
- How is it communicated?
- Does the event create meaningful player value?

### Communication

- What messages does a new player receive?
- When are reminders sent?
- How is notification fatigue avoided?

# 15. Competitive Intelligence Framework

Competitive research should be structured.

```text
OBSERVE
   ↓
DOCUMENT
   ↓
COMPARE
   ↓
IDENTIFY PATTERN
   ↓
FORM HYPOTHESIS
   ↓
VALIDATE WITH PLAYERS
   ↓
EXPERIMENT
```

The PM should avoid turning competitor research into a feature-copying exercise.

---

# 16. Product Decision Framework

When a competitor has a successful-looking feature, evaluate:

### Player Need

What player problem does it solve?

### Evidence

Do our players demonstrate the same problem?

### Strategic Fit

Does solving it support our product strategy?

### Experience Quality

Would the solution improve or complicate the player experience?

### Business Impact

Could it improve meaningful engagement or retention?

### Experimentability

Can we test the hypothesis safely?

# 17. Example Competitive Insight

### Observation

Multiple live-service games use structured goals and progression systems.

### Player Need

Players benefit from knowing what they can accomplish next.

### Internal Evidence

Moderate players:

- 45.02% of population
- 27.16% D7 retention
- 7.00 average sessions
- 7.88 average matches
- 8.37 average progression

High players:

- 44.27% D7 retention
- 15.04 average sessions
- 17.27 average matches
- 18.12 average progression

### Product Hypothesis

A clearer early progression loop may help players develop stronger repeat-play behavior.

### Proposed Solution

Week 1 Player Journey.

### Validation

A/B test D7 retention and supporting engagement metrics.

# 18. Competitive Analysis Limitations

This analysis does not include:

- Proprietary competitor telemetry
- Internal EA data
- Competitor revenue data
- Competitor retention data
- Player-level competitor experiments
- Controlled comparisons between games

Therefore, the analysis should be treated as a **strategic product framework**, not a quantitative market-share or competitor-performance report.

---

# 19. PM Takeaway

Good competitive analysis is not:

> "Competitor X has feature Y, so we should build feature Y."

It is:

> **"Competitor X demonstrates a product pattern that may solve a player need. Let's determine whether our players have the same need, validate it with research and data, and test the smallest useful solution."**
